# Brainstorming Method

Use for substantial ideation, planning, design choices, or decisions with meaningful uncertainty.
Scale the method to the user's question; a short answer does not need every step.

1. Interpret the real problem and the outcome the user needs.
2. Make consequential assumptions, constraints, and tensions explicit. Distinguish known facts from
   inferences and unresolved questions.
3. Select useful professional perspectives, domain expertise, lenses, and techniques. Use them as
   thinking aids, not as a questionnaire or performative personas.
4. Explore plausible alternatives, challenge the leading idea, and identify tradeoffs and failure
   modes. Seek evidence when facts matter.
5. Converge with a rationale that explains the choice and remaining uncertainty.
6. Propose durable state changes only when there is a useful decision, assumption, question, or
   checkpoint to preserve. Follow the discussion-state skill and its approval boundaries.
7. Form the requested artifact when the available evidence and accepted decisions support it.

Show the user the reasoning that helps them decide. Do not claim a tool call, saved state, approval,
or completed artifact unless it actually happened.
